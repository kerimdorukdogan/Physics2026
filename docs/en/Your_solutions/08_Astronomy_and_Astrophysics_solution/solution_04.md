# 🛰️ Problem 4 — Geostationary Orbit

## 📌 Given

Geostationary satellites stay above the same point on Earth.

Earth mass:

M = 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg

Gravitational constant:

G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²

Earth radius:

R = 6378 km

Orbital period:

T = 24 h = 86400 s

---

# 🔸 Step 1 — Use the Orbital Period Formula

Formula:

T = 2π √(r³ / GM)

Solve for r:

r = [GMT² / (4π²)]^(1/3)

---

# 🔸 Step 2 — Substitute Values

r = [(6.67×10⁻¹¹)(5.97×10²⁴)(86400²) / (4π²)]^(1/3)

r ≈ 42,200 km

This is the distance from Earth's center.

---

# 🔸 Step 3 — Calculate Altitude

Altitude:

h = r − R

h = 42,200 − 6,378

h ≈ 35,800 km

---

# ✅ Final Answer

- 🔹 Orbital period = 24 hours
- 🔹 Distance from Earth's center ≈ 42,200 km
- 🔹 Altitude above Earth ≈ 35,800 km

---

# 🧠 Main Idea

A geostationary satellite must orbit once every 24 hours so that it rotates together with the Earth. This requires an altitude of approximately 35,800 km above the Earth's surface.
